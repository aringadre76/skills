# Research a purchase

Recommend from the user's actual needs and current evidence. Research does not authorize an order or a cart change.

## Establish requirements

- Read the user's current owned-product inventory when available to avoid redundant recommendations. Use only the relevant device reference for compatibility.
- Extract the requested retailer, quantity, configuration, variant, condition, budget, and intended use. Ask when a missing hard constraint would change the result; for open-ended exploration, state reasonable assumptions and compare price tiers.
- For affordability claims, use current financial information only when the user asks and an authoritative source is available. Do not expose private balances in a public artifact.

## Compare candidates

- Verify exact listings, variants, seller, fulfillment, condition, included accessories, quantity, stock, and delivered cost from current retailer pages. Use the stated verification date.
- Use manufacturer documentation for specifications and credible independent reviews for performance or usability. Keep distinct variants and editions separate.
- Respect the requested retailer and configuration. Treat deal roundups and search snippets as leads, not proof of price or availability.
- If tax, shipping, stock, seller, or compatibility cannot be verified, state the unknown. Do not present the product as meeting a hard limit until the missing component is resolved.
- Offer a concise comparison of candidates that satisfy the constraints, followed by a reasoned choice and the tradeoff that matters.

## Check the recommendation

Reopen the selected listing and verify the exact variant, quantity, seller, price, and availability. Recheck compatibility and delivered-cost arithmetic. If live access fails, label the result provisional and state what remains unverified.
